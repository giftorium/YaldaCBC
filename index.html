The Night When Time Stands Still

Yalda is Iran’s winter solstice wake—a night stretched with fruit, flame, and verses until light returns.

What is Yalda?

Yalda falls on the longest night of the year. The word means “birth,” a nod to the sun’s return and the belief that dawn always follows patience.

The Sacred Pomegranate

Bowls of pomegranate and watermelon keep summer on the table. Every ruby seed feels like a small promise of warmth held for winter.

Enter Hafez: The Poet of Hearts

Hafez of Shiraz wrote layered ghazals about love, spirit, and everyday longing. His book is still opened when hearts need a direction.

The Magic of Fāl-e Hafez

For a Fāl-e Hafez, you make a wish and open the Divan at random. The poem on that page is read as the answer.

The Vigil of Light

Candlelight, dried fruit, and whispered legacies keep the room awake. No one wants to miss the moment darkness gives way.

Why It Matters Today

Yalda still matters because gathering matters. Sharing fruit, poems, and laughter keeps us linked to ancestors and to each other.
